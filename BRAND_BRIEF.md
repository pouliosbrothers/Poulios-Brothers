## About us

We're **Costi** (15) and **Leo** (11), two brothers working on climate issues with the support of our family. Our work so far includes organizing a beach cleanup on the Red Sea coast of El Gouna, Egypt; curating climate videos and UN reports for a young audience; and highlighting climate leaders we admire (Carl Sagan, Al Gore, Tom Chi, and many others).

We already own **pouliosbrothers.com** and are rebuilding it from scratch into a vibrant place where young people start and develop their environmental journey. Visitors will be able to:

- **Learn** the basics of key climate topics and sectors.
- **Get involved** in real projects, internships, and programmes worldwide.
- **Read** seminal research (Library) and the latest developments (Feed).
- **Give** to projects, charities, and causes.
- **Discover innovation** — the startups, investors, and people driving each sector.
- **Follow our story** — our blogs, adventures, and projects.
- **Post their project** — young people and climate organisations share their own work.

We'll build it gradually, but the brand needs to fit the full vision from day one.

## Why we're hiring you

The current site uses a GoDaddy template and is visually forgettable. That undersells our work. We want a real brand — a wordmark, type system, and color palette — that:

1. Feels like a place young people want to come back to — youthful and fun without being childish.
2. Reads as genuinely impressive and trustworthy to adults (college admissions officers, journalists, mentors, funders) and to climate organisations who'll list their work with us.
3. Holds together across many sections and content types (explainers, research, news, startups, causes, opportunities, our own story) without looking cluttered.
4. Scales from favicon to six-foot banner, from Instagram avatar to a printed one-pager.

This is the foundational identity for a long-running effort. We're not chasing trends; we're investing in something we'll use for years.

## Personality — what we are

- **Youthful but confident.** We're teens and tweens, but we're serious about the work.
- **Editorial, not corporate.** Think *The Atlantic*, *Monocle*, *MIT Technology Review* — typography doing the heavy lifting, confident spacing, nothing swoopy.
- **Sharp, not cute.** No mascots, no cartoons.
- **Optimistic but unflinching.** We avoid "sad polar bear" visual language, but we also don't pretend everything's fine.
- **Mediterranean / Greek as a subtle thread.** Our heritage is Greek (hence "Poulios"). The brand can carry a hint of Mediterranean warmth — but no literal Greek keys, columns, olive branches, or flag blues.

---

## Personality — what we're NOT

Please avoid:

- The default "eco" palette: leaf green + ocean blue + white.
- Hand-drawn leaves, globes, water droplets, sprouts, or sun rays.
- Cute kid or teen mascots.
- "Earth Day poster" aesthetics.
- Generic humanist sans + stock photo + gradient button = 2015 nonprofit look.
- "Inspiring" swooping gradients.
- "Wellness / planet-positive" Instagram aesthetic.

---

## Inspirations worth looking at

- *The Atlantic* and *Monocle* masthead typography — confident, editorial, not afraid of serifs.
- *Grist*, *Heatmap News*, *Carbon Brief* — climate outlets that avoid clichés.
- Display type families like **Söhne**, **Canela**, **Reckless**, **GT Sectra**, **Clash Display**, **Tiempos** — not requirements, just the direction (display-quality type, not default fonts).
- Mediterranean color palettes: sun-warmed terracotta, limestone, dusty blue, olive, deep aubergine, rich coral.

We're open to surprising directions. The best feedback we can imagine giving is: "we didn't know we wanted this until you showed us."

## Deliverables

### Identity
- Primary wordmark for "Poulios Brothers" — horizontal lockup.
- Optional compact mark or monogram that works solo (for favicon, avatar, merch).
- Light-mode and dark-mode variants for both.

### Color
- A palette of 5–7 colors with hex codes (primary, secondary, 2–3 accents, background/paper tones).
- Usage notes (what's body text, what's background, what's the reserved accent).

### Typography
- Headline + body pairing recommendation.
- Optional monospace for data / metadata feel.
- Google Fonts or open-source equivalents noted if the primary choice is licensed.

### Navigation system (optional — quote separately if out of budget)
- A simple way to tell sections and topics apart — e.g. a colour or icon per site section, and/or a small icon set for our 14 topics (energy, water, blue economy, nature, agrifood, circular economy, built environment, transport, industry, adaptation, policy, finance, philanthropy, careers). Must stay within the brand, not turn into a rainbow.

### Assets
- Favicon (SVG + 32×32, 192×192, 512×512 PNG).
- Social avatars (square crop, works for Instagram, TikTok, X / Bluesky).
- Open Graph card template (1200×630, editable).
- Email signature version.

### Documentation
- A 1–2 page brand guidelines PDF covering: the wordmark, clear space, minimum sizes, color system with hex codes, typography specs with sizes, and 2–3 "do / don't" examples.
- All working source files (Figma preferred; else Illustrator with layers labeled).

## Use cases the brand has to work in

- Website header, hero, and footer (light and dark modes).
- Cards and listings for many content types (explainers, research, news, startups, causes, opportunities).
- Favicon in a browser tab (must be recognizable at 16×16).
- Instagram and TikTok avatars.
- Printed one-pager for press kits.
- Eventually: T-shirts, stickers, and event signage.

## Process

1. **Day 1 — kickoff.** You confirm scope. Optional short kickoff call or everything can go over written messages, whichever works for you.
2. **Days 3–4 — directions.** You send 2–3 wordmark directions with preliminary color and type pairings. No need for polish — we want to see range.
3. **Days 5–7 — refine.** We pick a direction and request up to two rounds of revisions.
4. **Days 8–10 — delivery.** Final delivery: all files, the guidelines PDF, and the working source file.

Milestone payment works well for us — half at kickoff, half on final delivery.
